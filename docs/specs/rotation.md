# Rotation

The photograph toolbar must offer Rotate beside Crop with the same grouped split-button styling. Its menu must offer Straighten, Rotate Freehand, Rotate Left, Rotate Right and reset. Each choice must have a distinct icon in the menu and on the toolbar button. Freehand uses a plain circular arrow. Straighten adds crosshairs inside the arrow. Left and Right use mirrored circular arrows with a vertical stop bar beyond the arrowhead. Shift+R cycles Straighten, Freehand, Left and Right without changing the photograph. R selects the current rotation tool. Left and Right remain selected after applying their relative quarter turn. Selecting Left or Right from a button, menu or keyboard selects that tool without changing the photograph. Clicking the photograph applies one relative quarter turn. Command+Left and Command+Right apply a quarter turn directly to only the active photograph. Rotation tools operate only in the viewport. Browser thumbnail clicks retain ordinary selection behavior independently of the selected tool. Viewport selection belongs to Pointer V, as specified in [multi-edit](./multi-edit.md). The selected rotation icon must become the cursor over its image targets. Pointer V, Hand H and Enter finish editing. Enter returns to Pointer. Escape cancels a drag before leaving the tool. Space temporarily pans. Other on-image tools must remain mutually exclusive.

Straighten must support both click-drag-release and click-release-move-click-release. After a first click without a drag, retain the starting point and follow the pointer until the second click is released. Keep the photograph still while drawing and apply alignment only on the final release. Escape cancels an unfinished line while keeping Straighten active. Lines below 45 degrees align to horizontal. Lines above 45 degrees align to vertical, so either a horizon or a building edge can guide the correction. Freehand must rotate around the photograph center and display a guide grid while dragging. Fine rotation is limited to -45 through +45 degrees. Left and Right must add a relative 90-degree turn while preserving the fine angle and selected content. Reset must restore the original orientation independently of photographic adjustments. Double-click with Straighten or Freehand resets the fine angle and quarter turns to the camera orientation while preserving crop content.

The crop must remain inside the rotated photograph without blank corners. Automatically reduce its available area while keeping the chosen framing and aspect constraint. Show the exterior photograph dimmed while editing and the saved cropped result on leaving the framing tool. Report the proposed or saved fine angle in degrees using the shared upper-left [viewport badge](./gui-workspace.md) styling, in the same position as Before. Keep photographic dimensions in the existing pixel units.

Rotation belongs to the saved composition, alongside crop. Shared preview, photographic export, DNG, scope and picker sampling must use the same geometric mapping. Keep original processing buffers immutable and apply composition after photographic processing, before final resizing. Relative quarter turns must retain exact native sampling when no fine rotation or resizing is requested.

Crop and Rotate must share one untrimmed image plane, crop frame, exterior shading and photographic dimensions. Switching between them changes drag controls while preserving preview ownership, zoom and pan. It must not refit the image or request a replacement preview solely because the tool changed.

Dragging must update presentation using the existing texture without queuing a render for every pointer movement. Commit the draft on release through the ordinary request and sidecar path. Crop and rotation alone must reuse compatible completed photographic pixels rather than rerun their numerical pipeline or reload the source. Entering, leaving or committing framing must preserve the actual working resolution, including Thumb and Smart. Explicit preview-quality changes, detail zoom and photographic edits retain their normal processing rules. Composition updates must not cancel matching in-flight photographic work. Scope, navigator and browser preparation must preserve their artifact pairing.
