# Photographic finishing

Camera recipes must retain sensor calibration, corrections, noise cleanup, reconstruction and base camera appearance. Photographic operations with matching numerical meanings should be reusable across architectures. Existing methods and defaults must remain available while the owner evaluates alternatives.

The editor separates Tone, [Saturation](./saturation.md) and Color Range. Saturation owns its Reference/Universal method independently of Tone. The Universal response uses gamut-aware Oklab colorfulness with global Amount, Contrast and overlapping tonal saturation amounts. Color Range displays the existing linear Color Editor.


Universal tone must offer consistent contrast independent of the camera tone implementation. RAW exposure retains its existing interpretation. The approved spatial [HDR response](./tonal-ranges.md) must become available across photographic architectures. The tone-method choice must not switch HDR algorithms or move the camera adapter's HDR boundary. Classic, Merrill and Quattro, including Tiger, must share a linear photographic-edit stage before their camera tone response. Each camera adapter supplies primaries, nominal white, working geometry and the camera-curve-dependent HDR pivot. Required sensor calibration and reconstruction remain camera-owned. DNG baking follows the shared boundary below. Existing clipping before that boundary cannot be recovered by later edits.

Across Classic, Merrill and Quattro, Reference tone must expose the original Shadows and Highlights, and bypass the four HDR ranges and shared Local Contrast. Universal must expose Shadows, Highlights, Blacks, Whites, Brightness and Local Contrast, and bypass the original Shadows and Highlights. Exposure and contrast remain common. Method changes retain the inactive values without applying hidden edits. This choice does not change either HDR implementation or its processing position.

Universal tone must support the workflow Exposure → Dynamic range → Tone shaping. Show Exposure first, then Shadows, Highlights, Blacks and Whites, then one Brightness and Contrast. Keep Local Contrast and colorfulness controls separate. Do not expose the rejected three-band brightness experiment alongside spatial HDR.

Brightness must be neutral-centered with range -1 to +1. It must use an increasing rational curve in square-root brightness with black and nominal white anchored. The initial gain mapping is `g = 2^(2*amount)`. Measure normalized luminance after the selected Exposure gain, without applying Exposure twice to the samples. For `0 < Y < 1`, apply the scalar RGB gain `[g / (1 + sqrt(Y)*(g - 1))]^2`. Nonpositive luminance and luminance at or above nominal white pass through. Preserve RGB ratios, signed components, code scale and headroom without an extra clamp. Brightness runs after spatial HDR and before Local Contrast at the shared pre-camera-curve boundary. It is independent of Saturation and inactive under Reference tone. Zero must omit its dispatch and retain exact existing output. Sidecars, scoped presets, defaults, clipboard, processing-section bypasses and frozen batch settings must carry the value. DNG must bake it through the existing scalar or combined chromatic baking route. Expose its input/output comparison for inspection. Photographic strength and placement remain for owner review.

The [Color Editor](./color-editor.md) extends subject-color sensitivity with eight ranges and Hue/Saturation controls. Monochrome must retain its authored layer mixture and reconstruction. Color Editor Lightness controls must identify subject colors from an independent white-balanced color guide before monochrome mixing and creative saturation. Brightness adjustments apply to the resulting monochrome image. Color and Monochrome share this response on all supported architectures. Hue transitions must be smooth and nearly achromatic pixels protected. Zero sensitivity must avoid the guide branch. Guide generation must reuse the normal source and rendering operations, remain independent of the selected monochrome mixture, and participate in bounded interactive reuse.

Color and Monochrome must expose the same Color Editor Lightness controls and use the same pre-tone scalar brightness response. Classification and spatial stabilization must both use an independent cleaned neutral color guide, so the gain is independent of the monochrome mixture and retained creative edits. Apply one gain to all RGB components, preserving their ratios, signed samples and headroom. Full, reduced and viewport rendering must use matching guide geometry and sufficient filter support. Neutral settings must skip guide construction and response dispatches.

All supported Color and Monochrome recipes must apply active Universal saturation, Color Editor, spatial HDR, Brightness, Local Contrast and selected Universal sharpening in that order before the camera tone response. Saturation is inactive in sensor monochrome. DNG must bake their active combined scalar brightness gain into its existing technical samples. When chromatic Color Editor or Universal saturation edits are active, its inverse-profile-basis baking route replaces the scalar transfer as specified in the Color Editor requirements. Derive that gain from the same selected-look linear recipe used by native photographic rendering, including the camera-curve-dependent HDR pivot. For Lightness-only edits, transfer only brightness gain, retaining the established DNG technical color basis and camera appearance metadata. The Tone bypass suppresses its Exposure, contrast, HDR, Brightness and Local Contrast. Saturation has its own bypass. Color Editor has an independent bypass that retains its range settings. The Sharpening bypass suppresses its selected method in display and export. Neutral controls skip their work. Preserve the original X3F and editing sidecar for changing baked adjustments. DNG is an edited derivative, not an X3F replacement. Global tone presentation retains its existing DNG policy. Reference sharpening retains each camera recipe’s technical boundary. Universal sharpening must replace its creative contribution and bake into DNG through the shared linear stage. Required Quattro reconstruction and its unsigned-code limits remain in force.

Sharpening must offer Reference and Universal methods on all families. Universal must replace reference creative sharpening contributions and offer Amount, Radius, Threshold and Halo suppression. Necessary reconstruction remains independent. Neutral-centered multiscale Local Contrast is an accepted standalone photographic effect. It must remain distinct from denoising, reconstruction detail and sharpening, rather than replace any of them.

Quattro Smooth/Crisp combines residual restoration, local contrast and adaptive sharpening. Shared Local Contrast must not be presented as an equivalent reconstruction method. The owner accepted its distinct look after the comparison. Reference remains the default method. Its sharpening amount defaults to Auto when no amount is saved. Explicit saved manual amounts remain unchanged. Universal sharpening uses manual controls without Auto. Its pre-curve response requires photographic review separately from the retained Reference default.

All pixel operations run on the GPU through the existing typed graph and executor. Inspection, per-image persistence, section bypasses, frozen batch requests, Fast and Smart must share the same settings. Spatial operations must use sufficient regional support or explicitly run over a larger frame. Neutral operations must skip their work and preserve samples. Retained outputs remain immutable.

Remove redundant export-format labels from section headers now that both DNG and TIFF support the sections. Help must explain the difference between baked linear edits and embedded global presentation. Sharpening uses Method and Amount labels. Highlight correction and its Threshold belong in one parameter group. Indented text must use half the previous gap after its pipe without changing checkbox indentation.

All UI slider readouts, including sensitivity and hover values, must show at most three significant digits and two decimal places while retaining full stored precision. A value such as 0.007 must show 0.01 rather than adding a third decimal place. If two decimals would display a nonzero setting as zero, use compact scientific notation. Formatting and numeric-field sizing must agree so value changes do not expand the editor panel. Very small or large magnitudes may use compact scientific notation. Numeric widget construction must enforce the shared policy rather than require each control to attach a formatter. Both application themes, non-photographic numeric controls, custom parameter readouts and field sizing must follow it. Retain a regression check that prevents raw numeric widgets and formatter overrides outside the owning widget module.

Review must cover portraits, hair, fabric, foliage, sky and shadows. Validate arithmetic, finite headroom, neutral/reference output, cached/fresh agreement, regional agreement, TIFF inclusion and the documented DNG baking boundary. Report measured responsiveness and remaining visual decisions before selecting new defaults.
