## Findings

The manual-color follow-up found a processing-domain mismatch. The inspected raw chain applies the correction in white-balanced camera RGB before the camera matrix. Its default brightness weights are `.299/.587/.114`. The wider alternate chromatic gate belongs to processed inputs. The current adaptation instead selects candidates and applies the correction in linear sRGB after the selected camera matrix. Its RGB ratios, Euclidean chromatic distances and soft channel floors are not invariant under that conversion. Earlier scalar comparisons supplied matching parameters explicitly and did not establish caller equivalence.

A controlled test on the owner-supplied photograph held pixels, haze color, Amount and lower/upper calibration fixed. Changing only the processing basis and brightness weights increased the fraction above the gate's midpoint from 50.1% to 88.4% for an actual sky pick. A light manual color approximated from the screenshot changed from 42.0% to 99.2%. The camera-basis diagnostic removed much of the isolated patch-boundary pattern. Its response also agreed with 4,096 native point evaluations per case within `1.1e-6` normalized RGB. Inverting the known camera matrix after other processing is a controlled domain experiment, not a full photographic replay. The owner approved revising the earlier processing-space choice to move estimation, picking and correction into camera RGB. Stored manual colors and the swatch can retain sRGB transport.

A second inspected haze-removal approach estimates a spatial transmission map and refines it with an edge-aware guided filter. That explains broader spatial behavior without the same color gate, but it is a different algorithm. It does not justify adding smoothing to the recovered pointwise correction. Moderate Auto strength already looks useful to the owner and remains an acceptance case for the domain correction.

The owner proposed retaining the existing linear working primaries instead of converting to sRGB, and is open to camera coordinates if cost or photographic results support them. On the same photograph and actual sky pick at Amount 100, direct linear Adobe RGB raises gate-midpoint coverage only from 50.1% to 52.6%, compared with 88.4% in camera coordinates. For the screenshot-approximated light manual color, the three results are 42.0%, 56.8% and 99.2%. The direct-working diagnostic helps but retains more of the selective patch pattern.

Two isolated GPU timestamp runs on M1 Pro compare all three fused correction kernels. After five warm-ups, 24 interleaved repetitions per variant give median times of 0.55–0.56 ms at 2464×1632 and 2.08–2.11 ms at 4928×3264. Camera coordinates replace the existing sRGB matrix pair within the same pass. Direct working RGB removes that pair. Differences are within observed timing variation. These measurements exclude allocations, source preparation, estimation, image readback and presentation. Camera-domain candidate selection has not yet been timed and can depend on the changed candidate distribution.

The estimator uses a spatial dark-channel minimum and bounded candidate averaging. The correction is pointwise after the haze color and calibration are supplied. The local picker runs the estimator on a supported patch rather than reading one displayed pixel. Eighty synthetic configurations cover four sizes, five patterns and global/local preparation constants with unit and non-unit white balance. Maximum successful RGB error is below `3e-8`. Empty-candidate status agrees. This does not validate full photographic preparation or GUI replay.

Existing shared linear edits provide camera-supplied primaries, nominal white and geometry. Their DNG route already distinguishes scalar brightness transfer from chromatic baking. The new correction must use the latter when its color response is active. A stable whole-photo analysis basis is needed to avoid viewport-dependent Auto estimates. Selection must use the input before Dehaze to avoid feedback.

Use normalized linear RGB for the shared correction and retain signed residuals and highlight headroom. Whole-frame estimation needs a stable analysis artifact, not full-resolution correction. The owner corrected the initial Full-only restriction and approved quarter-pixel processing. Empty Auto estimates bypass correction. Empty manual picks retain the previous settings. Recovered preparation constants belong to their inspected input domain and require explicit adaptation before use here.

## Shared shadow-signal shoulder

The SD14 shadow crop remained finite and far from Classic’s signed-code wrap limit. At Amount 100, about 56,000 of 552,772 samples acquired negative blue before the camera response, versus 488 at Amount 12. Its lower signal and channel flooring made the remaining green conspicuous. This is a shared correction limitation, with visibility affected by input colors, reconstruction and camera response rather than a separate Merrill safeguard.

The owner approved one shared RGB protector. It shoulders the maximum positive relative channel loss and blends the full RGB correction toward the input. Existing negative channels remain signed; zero and negative Amount are unchanged. On the frozen SD14 crop, Amount 100 reduces negative-blue occurrences to 19 tiny residuals and substantially reduces strong green outliers. Amount 12 changes mean shadow luminance by about two percent. The near-zero numerical floor is one millionth of code white. Absolute input magnitude makes the shoulder continuous when a working RGB channel crosses zero, while retaining signed samples. Protection adds no analysis or image pass.

A full-size 4928×3264 Merrill original has median blend weights about 0.94 at Amount 12 and 0.69 at Amount 100. No new significant negative channels appear. One interleaved GPU sample with four warm-ups and 20 measured repetitions gives median correction times of 3.25 ms without protection and 3.92 ms with protection. This is about 0.67 ms in that run, excludes image preparation and estimation, and is not a cross-device latency guarantee. Independent native-equation fixtures explicitly disable the adaptation; nine synthetic GPU checks cover both the recovered operation and protection, including monochrome, signed samples, immutable inputs and code scales.

A second interleaved run of the final continuous-through-zero protector measured 7.19 ms without protection and 8.67 ms with it on the same full-size Merrill frame. Both runs show about 20 percent added kernel time, with absolute differences of 0.67 and 1.48 ms under different concurrent workloads. End-to-end estimation and reconstruction are excluded.
